# Troubleshooting

Symptom→fix lookup for OOM, NCCL hangs, DeepEP faults, parallelism-config rejections, NaN loss, and
attention-backend mismatches. Run `/debug` to route a live symptom to the exact helper.
A step that is slow rather than broken is a bottleneck question: read power, not util %
([GPU Training Theory §11](gpu-training-theory.md#watch-power-not-utilization)).

The trainer-side weight-sync errors are rows below; server-side symptoms are on
[Rollout Servers](../infrastructure/rollout-servers.md#troubleshooting), and Ray-actor, rollout and
environment symptoms on [Async GRPO → Troubleshooting](../training-methods/async-grpo/troubleshooting.md).

## First things to check

Most "it won't start" reports are environment, not code:

| Check | Why it bites |
|---|---|
| **Right image for the GPU.** `halo:blackwell` on B200 (SM100) / B300 (SM103), `halo:hopper` on H100/H200 (SM90). | The host has **no usable Python** — PyTorch, DeepEP, and Flash Attention live only inside the image. FA3 is Hopper-only, FA4 Blackwell-only; the wrong image gives import errors or kernel hangs. |
| **`--env-file .env` passed to `docker run`.** | The code does not auto-load `.env`. Without it, `WANDB_API_KEY` / `HF_TOKEN` / `AWS_DEFAULT_REGION` are unset, so a run that reports to W&B, pulls a gated repo, or reads an `s3://` dataset fails at that step — each needs your own account. Start from `.env.example`. |
| **No `poetry run` / `uv run` prefix.** | The image installs deps into the system interpreter, so `python` / `torchrun` / `accelerate` / `pytest` run directly. |
| **The scratch mount is actually large.** | The root filesystem is often small and shares the Docker image pool; a path named `/mnt` is not guaranteed to be a separate volume. Check `findmnt` / `df -h`, then point `HF_DATASETS_CACHE`, `TMPDIR`, `HALO_DATA_ROOT`, and logs at whichever mount has capacity. |

## Symptom → cause → fix

| Symptom | Cause | Fix | Deep page |
|---|---|---|---|
| `torch.OutOfMemoryError: CUDA out of memory` | Activations + weights + optimizer states exceed VRAM. | Gradient checkpointing on, lower `per_device_train_batch_size` / `max_length`, or move to a sharded mode. See [OOM](#oom). | [Debugging §3](debugging.md#3-gpu-memory-profiling) |
| `CUDA out of memory` after step 2 under `gradient_checkpointing_plan: auto` | The measured step did not hold the run's largest footprint: a pack fuller than any the step measured, EP routing drift past the 75% budget, or fragmentation growing past the measured ratio. | Relaunch into the same `output_dir` (resume or not): the unfinished plan's record makes the next plan checkpoint at least one step more. A timestamped `output_dir` gets a new directory, so no floor: pin it. `gradient_checkpointing_plan: off` keeps full checkpointing. | [GradientCheckpointingPlanCallback](../training-methods/callbacks.md#gradientcheckpointingplancallback) |
| **`gradient_checkpointing_plan: auto is refused for this run: …`** at startup | The plan relaxes per-layer checkpointing from one measured step of packed rows; the message lists every reason this run cannot take one: pipeline parallelism, `torch_compile`, `auto_find_batch_size`, a trainer that generates rollouts, unpacked or multimodal rows, activation offload, an explicit `every_n_layers`, model-level checkpointing, or checkpointing off. | Remove what the message names, or set `gradient_checkpointing_plan: off`. | [GradientCheckpointingPlanCallback](../training-methods/callbacks.md#gradientcheckpointingplancallback) |
| **`gradient_checkpointing_plan: auto measures CUDA allocator readings and needs a GPU`**, or **`The checkpointing plan record names every_n_layers=…`** at startup | The plan reads CUDA allocator stats, so a CPU run has nothing to measure. The record in `output_dir` names a policy the plan does not choose from at this layer count: another run's file, or an edited one. | Run on a GPU, or set `gradient_checkpointing_plan: off`. Remove a stale `_halo_run_plan.json` from `output_dir`. | [GradientCheckpointingPlanCallback](../training-methods/callbacks.md#gradientcheckpointingplancallback) |
| Job **hangs at step 0** or first cross-rank sync, no error, then a watchdog timeout | Collective mismatch — one rank issued a collective another never reached (divergent control flow, a straggler still in the dataloader). | Dump every rank's stack; the rank *not* in a collective is the culprit. See [NCCL hang](#nccl-timeout--hang). | [Debugging §4](debugging.md#4-diagnosing-multi-node-hangs) |
| Every GPU at **100% utilization but idle power**, all ranks spinning a CPU core, no NCCL error | A per-rank backward graph: a masked or empty row left disconnected from the loss let autograd prune that row's backward on one rank, so its FSDP2/EP collective never fired. | Keep every row connected to the loss (a value masked downstream is fine). Confirm with the NCCL flight recorder — mismatched collectives at one `collective_seq_id`. | [Debugging §4](debugging.md#4-diagnosing-multi-node-hangs) |
| **`trains or takes a gradient under reentrant gradient checkpointing, and takes no floating tensor`** at a training forward | A checkpointed layer holding a trainable parameter, or taking a gradient in another argument, gets no float positionally and no tensor as its first argument, so a reentrant checkpoint could give its outputs no gradient. Every family on the roster passes one. | Pass the hidden state positionally in the layer's caller, or train without gradient checkpointing. | [Data Parallelism](../parallelism/data-parallelism.md#tower-blocks-under-reentrant-checkpointing) |
| `None of the inputs have requires_grad=True. Gradients will be None` (torch `UserWarning`) on an image batch | A frozen tower's checkpointed block under reentrant checkpointing, a whole frozen tower or the blocks before the first trained one, with nothing to train. | None needed. A block holding an adapter or a trained parameter does not warn. | [Data Parallelism](../parallelism/data-parallelism.md#tower-blocks-under-reentrant-checkpointing) |
| **`This rank's batch holds no image while the vision tower trains under data parallelism`** at a training forward | Under `accelerate launch`, or with a frozen input embedding beside a trainable tower, gradient checkpointing off, and a trained decoder layer or ZeRO-3 (`unfreeze_layers_patterns` matching the tower but not the embedding, or an adapter reaching the tower, which the message names), a text rank's reduces cannot pair with an image rank's, and the step would hang to the collective timeout. | Launch with `torchrun`; or train the embedding, or turn checkpointing on; or drop the text-only rows. A fine-tune can also set `freeze_vision_tower: true` or train adapters; an adapter run, which refuses that knob, keeps `lora_target_modules` off the tower. | [Data Parallelism](../parallelism/data-parallelism.md#shard-groups) |
| `ValueError` at startup naming `expert_parallel_size=N on a single M-GPU NVLink domain forms K concurrent >2-rank DeepEP dispatch groups` (after the `parallelism config failed on … First (rank 0):` prefix under torchrun) | Racy single-domain multi-group EP: one NVLink domain, `ep_size > 2`, and `ep_group_size` smaller than the domain (`ep_size=4` on 8 GPUs). The combine barriers race FSDP2's DP-wide collectives, so the shape is refused at config time. | `ep_size=2`, `ep_size` = the domain, or raise `ep_size × expert_tp_size` to the domain (`ep4 + etp2` on 8). | **`EP capacity dedup: a MoE layer dispatched N tokens/rank, over the capacity C cached at forward generation G`** on one rank, its peers stuck in the dispatch | The forward reuses the first MoE layer's all-reduced capacity for every later layer, and a layer outgrew it: either the model's later MoE layers dispatch more tokens than its first, or this forward entered the backbone directly and opened no capacity scope of its own. | **`An expert id outside the local experts at ep_size 1, where the routing hands over only local ids`** in a `device-side assert triggered` (`ep_size: 1`, grouped GEMM) | At `ep_size` 1 nothing is dispatched, so every expert id the routing hands the expert compute must lie in `[0, num_experts)`: the routing sort counts the ids in that range and asserts they cover every slot, since an ID outside would misalign the expert groups. The ID came from the family's routing (a custom router, a changed top-k) or a [routing-replay](../training-methods/async-grpo/objective.md#routing-replay) mask row; engine masks are range-checked when they are assembled. | Not a DeepEP fault and not a config problem: report it with the model, the parallelism shape and whether routing replay was on. | [Grouped GEMM](../optimization/grouped-gemm.md) |
| `ImportError` / `NVSHMEM` / undefined symbol on `import deep_ep` | Wrong NVSHMEM package, or DeepEP used without a GPU / the right image. | Run inside the image ([DeepEP faults](#deepep-build--runtime-faults)). | [DeepEP](../infrastructure/deepep.md) |

| **`FlashAttention-4's varlen kernels are not validated with attention logit softcapping`** at load (Gemma 2), or **`attn_implementation='flash_attention_4' cannot train this model on packed, padding-free or padded rows`** (Gemma 2 27B; the resolver moves a head_dim-256 pin to FA2) | FA4's varlen kernels are not validated with a softcap; packed, padding-free and padded rows (more than one per batch), training or evaluation, all reach them. | Unset: auto-handled, the run takes FA2. | **`A softcapped attention call reached FlashAttention-4's varlen kernels`** in the first forward (Gemma 2 on a pinned `flash_attention_4`, a padded script such as DPO, KTO, SMPO, reward, classification or GRPO) | The batch unpads to FA4's varlen entry point, which refuses a softcap (not validated there). ## OOM

Peak memory is dominated by saved activations, not weights. Cut it in this order:

1. **Gradient checkpointing on** — trades recompute for activation memory. Turn it off only when the
   batch already fits; the recompute costs wall-clock.
2. **Lower `per_device_train_batch_size` or `max_length`** — both scale activations linearly.

3. **Move to a sharded mode.** Dense model that OOMs in FSDP2 → add node-local TP. MoE model that
   OOMs → EP (experts across ranks) or pure ETP (`ep_size=1`, expert FFN sharded).

A large **"reserved by PyTorch but unallocated"** figure in the OOM message points at allocator
fragmentation rather than at the steps above: launch with
`PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`. Under EP it costs throughput, so set it there only to fit
([measured](../optimization/throughput-benchmarks.md#allocator-expandable-segments)).

To find *what* holds memory, dump a CUDA snapshot and drag it onto
[memory_viz](https://pytorch.org/memory_viz) ([Debugging §3](debugging.md#3-gpu-memory-profiling)).

## NCCL timeout / hang

A hang is almost always a collective mismatch: one rank issues an all-reduce / all-gather / barrier
that another never reaches, so NCCL blocks until the watchdog fires.

```bash
python scripts/profiling/py_spy_diag.py dump   # py-spy stacks for EVERY rank on the node
```

Attaching needs ptrace access ([Debugging §1c](debugging.md#1c-py-spy--cpu-flame-graph-dataloader--python-stalls)). Dumps land in `$HALO_DATA_ROOT/profiling/py_spy/stacks`, and
the rank *not* in a collective is the culprit.
For the definitive "which collective did rank K miss?", enable the NCCL flight recorder
(`TORCH_NCCL_TRACE_BUFFER_SIZE`, `TORCH_NCCL_DUMP_ON_TIMEOUT`).

One class of hang is refused up front instead: a job whose ranks disagree on an EP, timeout, save, attention
or matmul-precision knob (`HALO_EP_CAPACITY_DEDUP`, the DeepEP wire parameters, `HALO_GRAD_BUCKET_MB`,
`HALO_CHECKPOINT_WRITE_THREADS`, `HALO_CHECKPOINT_WRITERS_PER_NODE`, `DIST_NCCL_TIMEOUT_MINUTES`, `DIST_STORE_TIMEOUT_HOURS`,
`DIST_FS_PROBE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, `FLASH_ATTENTION_DETERMINISTIC`, `HALO_FLEX_SLIDING`,
`HALO_FP32_MATMUL_PRECISION`) raises
`Rank-uniform toolkit environment` in distributed setup, before the weight load — a per-node `--env-file`
rollout that reached only some nodes would otherwise diverge the collective counts from the second MoE layer
on, or load a head_dim-256 model on a different attention backend per rank, naming nothing.

Raise the watchdog for large cross-node all-to-all, or for a gathered save on storage that writes one
part (`save_max_shard_size`) slower than it: a gathered EP/TP save holds the non-writers in the next
chunk's gather while the writer flushes a part. The joins after a save's unbounded writes are exempt:
every rank's optimizer shard, a sharded EP save's per-rank shards, a gathered save's last part and
index and each pipeline stage's parts are joined over the c10d store (`DIST_STORE_TIMEOUT_HOURS`,
default 4 h). So are main-first work (model downloads, dataset load, tokenize/pack) and the
node-batched model load (`max_concurrent_loading`): the peers wait on the store, not inside a
collective.

```bash
export DIST_NCCL_TIMEOUT_MINUTES=60   # default 30; PyTorch's own default is 10
```

Full procedure: [Debugging §4](debugging.md#4-diagnosing-multi-node-hangs).

## DeepEP build / runtime faults

DeepEP is required for EP — there is no NCCL fallback. The recurring failure modes:

- **`import deep_ep` fails (NVSHMEM / undefined symbol).** On Blackwell + PyTorch 2.11+cu130,
  NVSHMEM ships as `nvidia-nvshmem-cu13`; installing `nvidia-nvshmem-cu12` clobbers the headers. Use
  the prebuilt image.

- **`Assertion exception ... != NCCL_GIN_TYPE_NONE` / "NCCL GIN is unavailable" at buffer init on
  cross-node EP (EFA).** Proxy GIN could not come up on at least one node. See
  [DeepEP → AWS EFA](../infrastructure/deepep.md#expert-parallelism-over-aws-efa).

    Check, on **every** node: the `gdrdrv` kernel module is loaded (`ls /dev/gdrdrv`; the host must
    install the gdrcopy driver), the container was started with `--device /dev/gdrdrv`, and
    `NCCL_GIN_TYPE=2` is exported. One node missing any of the three fails the whole job with this
    assertion while node-local EP on the same machine runs fine; NCCL's log names the gap as
    `Failed to initialize GDRCopy`.

    A host can load the module without creating the device node (`grep gdrdrv /proc/devices` prints a
    major number, `ls /dev/gdrdrv` fails). Create the node on the host, or inside the container:
    start it with `--device-cgroup-rule='c <major>:0 rwm'` instead of `--device /dev/gdrdrv`, and run
    `mknod -m 666 /dev/gdrdrv c <major> 0` before `torchrun`.

- **`CUBLAS_STATUS_EXECUTION_FAILED` / DeepEP `combine.hpp` `CUDA_ERROR_LAUNCH_FAILED` + Xid 43 on
  several ranks at once, single-node EP.** Before reading it as a kernel or hardware fault, grep the
  full log for `OutOfMemoryError`.

    A rank-local OOM inside a distributed step abandons the DeepEP barrier its peers are waiting in
    (`DeepEP NVLink barrier timeout` lines). As its context tears down, the peers die on launch
    failures inside whatever kernel is on-stream. The OOMing rank's traceback is the primary failure
    and is easily buried under the collateral.

    The trainer logs a `RANK n: ... THIS RANK IS THE PRIMARY FAILURE` banner for it, and warns after
    the first optimizer step when a rank's peak sits above ~92% of its device.

    The failing rank exits without entering distributed teardown (both halves are themselves
    collectives, and its peers are still inside the step's own), so torchrun reaps the job in
    seconds rather than at the NCCL watchdog.

    On MoE the usual cause is routing skew. A cold (unbalanced) router concentrates dispatch buffers
    and expert activations on hot ranks, and under gradient checkpointing every MoE layer's saved
    dispatch/combine results scale with it.

    Under `bias_update` the skew (watch `moe/load_max`) falls over the first few hundred steps, so a
    batch shape that OOMs at cold start can fit when resumed from a balanced checkpoint. Cold-start
    at the smaller batch, or lower `per_device_train_batch_size` / `max_length`.

- **`DeepEP receive counts disagree with the received expert ids` in a `device-side assert triggered`
  on one rank, its peers then timing out in DeepEP's barrier.** The dispatch's receive counts do not
  match the expert ids it delivered. See
  [DeepEP → Runtime errors](../infrastructure/deepep.md#runtime-errors).

- **`Dispatch CPU wait ... received count 0`, or Xid 109 `CTX SWITCH TIMEOUT` → Xid 43 /
  `unspecified launch failure`, on cross-node EP (EFA).** The dispatch exceeded the proxy-GIN
  per-rank payload ceiling and wedged in transit. See
  [DeepEP → AWS EFA](../infrastructure/deepep.md#expert-parallelism-over-aws-efa).

    The Xid 43 / launch-failure form surfaces in whatever kernel is on-stream (often the expert
    GEMM) and reads like a compute bug, but the trigger is the oversized dispatch. A declared
    `per_device_train_batch_size × max_length` (under PP, one microbatch's rows × `max_length`) over `8192` tokens/rank
    (`HALO_DEEPEP_GIN_MAX_TOKENS_PER_RANK`) is rejected at config time, before the model loads. That
    gate reads the train batch only, so an evaluation over the ceiling (`per_device_eval_batch_size`
    defaults to 8) passes it, starts training, and raises at the dispatcher's buffer-sizing check on
    its first oversized batch. A wedge with no raise means the ceiling was disabled or raised. Lower
    `per_device_train_batch_size`, `per_device_eval_batch_size` or `max_length`, or use
    `ep_scope=node` with DP across nodes.

`CUDA_DEVICE_MAX_CONNECTIONS=1` is baked into the images as `ENV` (the driver latches it at the
`deep_ep` import's `cuInit`, so a Python `os.environ` write is too late). Measured effect:
[DeepEP](../infrastructure/deepep.md).

## Turning off a fused kernel

The toolkit's fused kernels, the MoE ones, the hyper-connection ones and the flex-sliding attention, have no runtime fallback: one that fails to compile or launch raises. A MoE or hyper-connection kernel raises at its first forward or backward. The flex-sliding attention compiles at model load when the world size is above 1, so it raises there, and on a single rank at its first masked call longer than the window or one tile. They are validated on B300; on other GPUs, turn off the one that fails.

- **Flex-sliding attention** (models with sliding-window layers or heads wider than 256, such as Step-3.7 and Gemma 4, on a run resolved to `sdpa`): `HALO_FLEX_SLIDING=0` sends every attention layer back to plain SDPA.
- **Masked matmul attention** (GLM-5 Next's DSA layers on `sdpa`, `halo/kernels/masked_softmax.py`, warmed at model load at world size above 1, otherwise compiled at the first backward): `HALO_DSA_MATMUL=0` keeps stock SDPA.
- **Fused GLU** (`halo/kernels/fused_glu.py`, every expert and dense-MLP combine, grouped and loop paths alike): `HALO_FUSED_GLU=0` runs each combine through its eager form on CUDA too, and the EP layers' construction lines read `glu_combine=eager`. On CPU tensors, and for an activation the kernels do not compute exactly, the eager form runs anyway.
- **Hyper-connection kernels** (`halo/kernels/hyper_connection.py`, GLM-5 Next and DeepSeek-V4): `HALO_HC_KERNELS=0` runs upstream's forwards unpatched.
- **Native RMSNorm** (a Liger role): `liger_kernel_config: {rms_norm: false}` keeps the family's own norm, and `use_liger_kernel: false` turns off every Liger role.
- **Fused weighted un-permute and routing sort** (`halo/kernels/moe_permute.py` and the counting sort of `halo/kernels/moe_route_sort.py`, on every grouped-GEMM path): no switch of their own. `use_grouped_gemm: false` avoids both by running the per-expert loop instead of the grouped path. That costs throughput, most with many local experts per rank ([Grouped GEMM](../optimization/grouped-gemm.md#grouped-vs-the-loop-path)).
